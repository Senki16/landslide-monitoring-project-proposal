Seeing landslides before they close the road.

A monitoring platform that combines NASA satellite data, a dynamic risk matrix and computer vision to warn authorities about potential landslides on the Medellín–Bogotá highway (Route 60) in Antioquia, Colombia.

The problem

Colombia pays for landslides after they happen.

Landslides in geologically critical areas threaten the safety, economy and integrity of the people who depend on the road network. Public spending is still concentrated on response rather than prevention.

11,800+landslides recorded in Colombia over the last 100 years (UNGRD, 2020)
1,393cases in Antioquia, the department with the most records
40 %of deaths from natural events in Colombia are caused by landslides (Ayala & Ospino, 2023)
80 %+of Colombians live in areas of “high” mass-movement risk (SGC, 2018)

Why Route 60 at Alto de Pavas

San Francisco is the municipality with the most landslides in Antioquia (DAGRAN, 1992–2022). The study focus is a curved section with a back slope at Alto de Pavas (5.950459, −74.861388), km 87 towards Medellín, on the San Luis – Cocorná toll section operated by Devimed. Curves with cut slopes concentrate drainage problems and geological stress.

Heavy seasonal rainfall, limestone mining, livestock farming and unplanned roadside housing all add pressure. In October 2020 landslides in San Luis left 2 dead and 3 injured; on 22 June 2024 a slide fully closed the highway.

The solution

A geographic monitoring platform with Industry 4.0 technology.

Selected among four alternatives with 68 of 75 points. It builds on NASA's LHASA approach — rainfall plus terrain susceptibility — and adds computer vision on satellite imagery.

Alternatives evaluated

Six specific objectives

  1. Database. Design and implement a geospatial and meteorological database that stores and manages data in real time.
  2. Dynamic risk matrix. Integrate terrain stability, weather and other variables to identify vulnerable areas and prioritize prevention.
  3. Neural network. Train an algorithm to detect critical patterns and predict landslides, improving alert accuracy.
  4. Monitoring platform. Real-time visualization, automatic reports and configurable alerts.
  5. Pilot tests. Iterate the system with real data to ensure effectiveness and scalability.
  6. Reports. Automated technical recommendations for road and disaster-prevention managers.
